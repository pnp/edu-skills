# Create Bell Ringer

Create a focused opening activity that students can begin with minimal teacher explanation. Use the current presentation's Slide Master, assigned layout, theme colors, and theme fonts so the result remains editable and consistent with the lesson deck.

## Gather the Instructional Context

1. Work on the current slide unless the educator asks for a new opening slide.
2. Identify the subject, learner age or grade, lesson topic, and learning objective.
3. Prefer a Teach-generated lesson plan when the educator selects, attaches, or references one. Use selected slide content, speaker notes, other educator-provided source material, or the user's prompt when no Teach plan is available.
4. Determine the intended duration. Default to five minutes when no duration is supplied, and label that assumption in the completion report.
5. Determine whether the activity should:
   - Retrieve prior learning
   - Check readiness for today's lesson
   - Surface a likely misconception
   - Prompt observation or interpretation of supplied material
   - Preview a new concept without requiring untaught knowledge
   - Invite a brief reflection or quick write
6. Ask for missing context only when subject, learner level, or topic cannot be inferred. Do not ask for optional preferences that can be handled with a sensible default.

## Use a Teach Lesson Plan

When a Teach-generated lesson plan is available, read it as the primary instructional source and preserve its intent. Do not modify the source document.

1. Map the lesson-plan sections before drafting:
   - Lesson title
   - `Overview` metadata, including grade, subject, and total duration
   - `Standards`
   - `Objectives`
   - `Materials`, including accessibility supports
   - Timed phases under `Lesson Outline`
2. Treat the first Lesson Outline phase as the preferred Bell Ringer source, especially when it is labeled `Hook`, `Opening`, `Launch`, `Engage`, `Warm-Up`, `Do Now`, or similar.
3. Adapt the first phase into a self-starting student task while retaining its question, stimulus, vocabulary, misconception, and intended connection to prior learning.
4. Use the Objectives to keep the Bell Ringer aligned with the lesson. Select the objective most directly supported by the opening phase rather than trying to cover every objective.
5. Use the Overview grade and subject to set language, expected background knowledge, and response length.
6. Use only materials that are already available in the plan or on the slide. If the opening depends on a missing image, card, model, passage, or other resource, identify it instead of inventing it.
7. Keep the Bell Ringer shorter than the full opening phase when needed. A ten-minute Teach hook may become a five-minute entry task followed by teacher-led discussion.
8. Do not repurpose the plan's `Exit Ticket`, `Reflection`, or final assessment as the Bell Ringer unless the educator explicitly requests retrieval from a previous lesson.
9. When no separate warm-up or prior-knowledge section exists, infer that function from the first timed phase and state that mapping in the completion report.

## Choose the Activity

Use one primary activity per slide. Select the simplest format that supports the objective:

- **Retrieval:** one to three short questions based on previously taught content.
- **Solve and explain:** one bounded problem plus a short reasoning prompt.
- **Notice and wonder:** a supplied image, diagram, quotation, graph, or passage with two observation prompts.
- **Misconception check:** one plausible claim or worked example that students evaluate and correct.
- **Quick write:** one specific prompt with a response length, such as two sentences or three bullets.
- **Predict:** one evidence-based prediction students can revisit during the lesson.
- **Sort or rank:** three to five supplied items with a clear organizing rule.
- **Vocabulary activation:** a brief connection, example/non-example, or word-use task rather than copying definitions.

Do not combine several unrelated tasks to fill the slide. Add one optional extension labeled `If you finish early` only when it reinforces the same objective.

## Write the Student-Facing Content

1. Use the heading `Bell Ringer` or the educator's preferred label, such as `Do Now` or `Warm-Up`.
2. Write directions as direct student actions. Include the expected response format and where students should record their work.
3. Keep the primary task brief enough to complete within the stated time.
4. Show the lesson objective in student-friendly language when requested or when it clarifies why the task matters. Do not turn the objective into a large second activity.
5. Use language appropriate to the learner age. Define essential unfamiliar terms, but do not over-explain content the activity is meant to retrieve.
6. When generating a prompt from a topic, avoid unsupported facts, quotations, statistics, standards codes, or source claims. Use supplied source material for tasks that depend on exact evidence.
7. Preserve mathematical notation, scientific units, historical uncertainty, and source wording accurately.
8. Do not reveal the correct response on the opening state of the slide.

## Build the Slide

1. Preserve the slide title placeholder, margins, existing background, theme, and school or district branding.
2. Give the primary task the largest content area. Keep instructions, objective, duration, and extension visually subordinate.
3. Use a simple structure:
   - Activity label and short task title
   - Student directions
   - Primary prompt or supplied stimulus
   - Visible time expectation
   - Optional `If you finish early` extension
4. Keep all response choices, labels, axes, excerpts, and relevant evidence visible at a readable size.
5. Use native editable text and shapes. Do not flatten instructional content into an image.
6. Use a meaningful supplied image, graph, quotation, passage, or diagram only when the activity depends on it. Do not add decorative stock imagery.
7. Add source attribution in speaker notes or visibly near the stimulus when required.
8. Avoid decorative cards, gradients, 3D effects, excessive icons, and motion that delays students from starting.

## Optional Timing

- Show a static duration label by default, such as `5 minutes`.
- When the educator requests a functioning visual countdown, apply the `create-countdown-timer` skill using native shapes, Motion Path animation, and Animation Pane duration.
- Place the timer where it does not cover the task or stimulus.
- Do not add VBA, macros, embedded video, animated GIFs, add-ins, or an external web timer.
- Do not imply that a static duration label is a live timer.

## Optional Answer or Discussion Reveal

When the educator requests an answer, model, or discussion prompt:

1. Keep it hidden when the slide opens.
2. Add a visible `Reveal response` control.
3. Reveal a concise correct response, worked example, success criteria, or discussion guidance with `Appear` or a short `Fade` triggered by that control.
4. For open-ended tasks, show one reasonable example or discussion criteria rather than presenting a single response as the only acceptable answer.
5. Put detailed teacher notes, accepted variants, misconceptions to watch for, and facilitation guidance in speaker notes.
6. If trigger animation cannot be created reliably, place the answer on the next slide and add clear `Show response` and `Back to Bell Ringer` links.

## Accessibility and Teaching Quality

- Keep text readable from the back of a classroom. Shorten content or use a second source slide rather than shrinking text excessively.
- Use sufficient contrast and do not rely on color alone for instructions, correctness, or emphasis.
- Add descriptive alt text to every meaningful image, graph, diagram, and interactive control.
- Use a logical reading order: heading, directions, prompt or stimulus, duration, extension, reveal control.
- Make answer-reveal controls visible, keyboard-orderable, and large enough for touch.
- Avoid flashing, rapid animation, countdown anxiety cues, and unnecessary sound.
- Do not require students to disclose sensitive personal information in a public classroom response.
- Keep the task understandable in edit view, printed handouts, and PDF exports even when optional reveal behavior is unavailable.

## Validation

Review the slide at fit-to-window and, when interactive, in Slide Show mode.

1. Confirm the subject, grade level, topic, objective, and duration match the educator's request or reported assumptions.
2. When using a Teach lesson plan, confirm the task comes from the first suitable Lesson Outline phase and aligns with a stated Objective. Report any inferred mapping, such as treating the first Hook as prior-knowledge activation.
3. Confirm students can understand what to do, where to respond, and how much to write without verbal clarification.
4. Confirm the task fits the stated duration and contains one coherent primary activity.
5. Confirm every factual statement, quotation, data point, answer, and source attribution is supported by the supplied material or common foundational knowledge appropriate to the request.
6. Confirm no answer is visible in the opening state.
7. If an answer reveal is used, confirm the control reveals only the intended response and any fallback links work.
8. If a live countdown is used, test it according to the `create-countdown-timer` validation requirements.
9. Confirm no text, image, graph, label, or control is clipped, overlapped, or too small.
10. Confirm contrast, alt text, reading order, keyboard access, and touch-target size.
11. Report the activity type, duration, response format, timer mode, answer mode, source material used, assumptions, and any content still needed.
