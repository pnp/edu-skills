# Create Differentiated Deck

Create a separate learner-ready version of the open lesson deck. Preserve the lesson's instructional goal, factual meaning, sequence, and visual identity while changing access, support, pacing, or challenge for the requested learners.

## Protect the Source

1. Read the complete presentation, including slide titles, body content, speaker notes, sources, and existing accessibility information.
2. Do not overwrite the original presentation. Create a separate presentation or clearly labeled duplicate version.
3. Preserve the original deck's Slide Master, layouts, theme colors, theme fonts, branding, slide size, and source attribution.
4. Use native editable PowerPoint text, shapes, tables, charts, and images. Do not flatten instructional content into screenshots.
5. Keep an unchanged source slide available when a transformation could alter nuance, evidence, mathematical notation, scientific units, quotations, or assessment validity.

## Identify the Instructional Invariants

Before changing slides, identify and hold constant:

- Subject, grade or learner level, and lesson topic
- Standards and learning objectives
- Essential questions, success criteria, and required vocabulary
- Factual claims, examples, data, quotations, and sources
- Required student task and response format
- Assessment construct: the knowledge or skill the task is intended to measure
- Lesson sequence, unless the educator explicitly asks to reorganize it

Do not lower the learning objective merely because access supports are added. Do not remove essential disciplinary vocabulary; explain and reinforce it.

## Choose the Differentiation Profile

Use the educator's named learner need or requested mode. **Default mode:** when none is supplied, create one general scaffolded-access version and report that assumption. Do not infer a disability, diagnosis, reading level, home language, or individual student's needs from names, appearance, or unrelated information.

Apply only the modes that support the stated need:

- **Language access / multilingual learners:** shorten sentence structure, clarify references, define essential vocabulary, add sentence frames or word banks, and pair text with meaningful visuals. Preserve content-specific terms and avoid childish language.
- **Reading support:** chunk dense text, use descriptive headings, reduce unnecessary wording, clarify pronouns and transitions, and split long passages across slides when needed. Preserve evidence students must read and analyze.
- **Executive-function support:** add a visible agenda, numbered steps, one action per step, time expectations, completion checks, and consistent task placement.
- **Additional scaffolding:** add a model, worked example, cue, partially completed organizer, guided question, or gradual release sequence. Keep the final task aligned with the original objective.
- **Vocabulary support:** add student-friendly explanations, context examples, non-examples when useful, pronunciation support when supplied, and repeated use in the lesson. Do not replace the disciplinary term with an imprecise synonym.
- **Enrichment:** increase depth, transfer, comparison, evidence use, ambiguity, or student choice. Do not use extra repetitive work as enrichment and do not introduce unsupported advanced content.
- **Presentation access:** improve contrast, reading order, alt text, whitespace, consistent navigation, and visual clarity without treating accessibility fixes as a substitute for instructional differentiation.

## Plan the Alternate Deck

1. Create a slide inventory with each source slide's purpose: launch, instruction, model, guided practice, independent practice, discussion, assessment, or closure.
2. For each slide, decide whether to:
   - Keep it unchanged
   - Revise language or layout
   - Add a scaffold on the same slide
   - Split it into a short sequence
   - Add a support slide before or after it
3. Keep the original order by default. If a dense slide is split, preserve its instructional position and make the progression obvious.
4. Avoid adding support to every slide mechanically. Change only what improves access or challenge for the selected profile.
5. Name the output clearly, such as `[Original title] - Scaffolded`, `[Original title] - Language Support`, or `[Original title] - Enrichment`.

## Transform the Slides

1. Preserve recognizable slide titles where possible so educators can move between versions.
2. Put the learner action first. Directions must state what to do, where to respond, how much to produce, and any time expectation.
3. Replace dense paragraphs with concise chunks, not disconnected fragments. Keep relationships among ideas explicit.
4. Use one main instructional purpose per slide. Add continuation slides instead of shrinking text below the deck's normal readable size.
5. Use meaningful diagrams, images, examples, organizers, and icons only when they clarify the content. Preserve supplied media and crop unless a change is needed for comprehension or accessibility.
6. Add visual labels, patterns, or text in addition to color when encoding categories or steps.
7. Put teacher-facing guidance, accepted responses, facilitation notes, and explanations of added supports in speaker notes rather than in the student's main reading path.
8. Preserve citations and add attribution for any new sourced material. Do not invent standards, quotations, statistics, examples presented as facts, or source claims.

## Preserve Assessment Validity

- Keep the same assessed knowledge or skill unless the educator explicitly requests a modified objective.
- Clarify directions and presentation barriers without giving away the answer.
- Prevent answer leakage: no added support may reveal a correct response or remove the reasoning the assessment is intended to measure.
- Do not simplify source evidence when close reading of that evidence is the assessed skill.
- Do not add hints, word banks, examples, or sentence frames that disclose a selected-response answer or eliminate required reasoning.
- For enrichment, extend the same objective through transfer or deeper evidence rather than moving to an unrelated topic.
- Label any change that alters task difficulty, response mode, scoring expectation, or the construct being assessed for educator review.

## Optional Version Map

When the educator requests traceability or when many slides change, add a teacher-only version map at the end of the alternate deck or in speaker notes. For each changed slide, record:

- Source slide number and title
- Differentiation mode applied
- What changed
- What remained instructionally fixed
- Any educator decision still required

Do not expose student names, diagnoses, accommodation plans, or other sensitive information in the deck or version map.

## Accessibility

- Use sufficient contrast and readable text sizes.
- Add descriptive alt text to meaningful images, charts, diagrams, and controls.
- Maintain a logical reading order on every slide.
- Do not rely on color, position, sound, or animation alone to convey meaning.
- Keep instructions and content understandable in edit view, Slide Show, printed handouts, and PDF export.
- Avoid flashing, unnecessary motion, decorative clutter, and images that reinforce stereotypes.

## Validation

Review the complete alternate deck at fit-to-window and in Slide Show mode.

1. Confirm the original presentation remains unchanged and the alternate version is clearly named.
2. Confirm full-deck coverage: every source slide is represented, intentionally split, or explicitly documented as omitted.
3. Confirm standards, objectives, essential content, vocabulary, factual meaning, sources, and lesson sequence remain accurate.
4. Confirm the selected differentiation profile is applied consistently without assuming diagnoses or lowering rigor by default.
5. Compare assessments with the source deck and flag any change to difficulty, response mode, scoring, or assessed construct.
6. Confirm directions state the action, response location, expected amount, and timing when relevant.
7. Confirm scaffolds support the task without revealing answers or replacing required student thinking.
8. Confirm enrichment adds depth, transfer, evidence, or choice rather than repetitive workload.
9. Confirm no text, image, chart, table, label, or control is clipped, overlapped, distorted, or too small.
10. Confirm contrast, alt text, reading order, navigation, and print/PDF comprehension.
11. Report the output name, learner profile or mode, slides changed, slides added or split, assessment-impact flags, source material used, assumptions, and decisions still needed.
